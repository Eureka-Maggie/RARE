# Short screenplay: Beyond the Canvas
**Genre**: Women's growth / Artistic creation / Interwoven reality / Emotional conflict  
**Duration**: Approximately 6 minutes  
**Plot points**: 6  
**Directorial sensibility**: Hirokazu Kore-eda × Beckett's realism: restraint, with psychological tension inside everyday detail

---

## Overall tone
- **Central idea**: When love tries to discipline creative freedom, authentic choice occurs at the moment one stops seeking to be understood.
- **Visual style**: Cool everyday realism, ink and sketch textures, visual conflict as real life encroaches on artistic space.
- **Narrative approach**: Linear, intercutting creative logs and fragments of life; divisions in time show accumulating conflict and its eruption.
- **Point of view**: First-person interior perspective, centered on Lin Ran's observations and reflection, restrained and calmly subjective.
- **Music**: Minimal piano with ambient sound and occasional fragments of electronic beats, suggesting creative desire's undercurrent rubbing against reality.
- **Emotional arc**: Calm → tension → conflict → betrayal → calm reflection → quiet choice → lingering freedom.
- **Devices**:
  1. Parallel life and creative scenes share time but oppose each other in content.
  2. Unfinished works, torn sketches, and doodles at love letters' edges carry emotion.
  3. Natural, desaturated everyday scenes contrast with high-contrast cold gray-blue painting scenes, exposing displaced values.
  4. Silence speaks: still pauses without dialogue at turning points let emotion develop in empty shots.

## Characters
| Name | Identity | Appearance | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lin Ran | 30, independent graphic artist and freelance illustrator | Black cotton-linen work trousers, khaki cardigan; pencil marks on wrists; worn metal ring on left ring finger | Introverted, sensitive, seeks formal beauty, expresses feelings through drawing, keeps distance in intimacy |
| Zhou Ran | 31, advertising planner, her boyfriend | Slightly plump; navy shirt with coffee-stained collar, slightly curly hair, ear stud | Rational, practical, values stability; defines relationships through plans and words, cannot understand creation's unpredictability |

## Screenplay

### S01: Morning studio · INT. · Day ｜ [00:00–00:45]

*“Last night I dreamed the white wall cracked. Sunlight slipped through onto the woman in the gray dress in my painting. She was smiling, as if waiting for me to wake.”*

Lin Ran sits at the easel, carbon pen in her left hand, right hand stroking an unfinished portrait. Charcoal outlines a woman's profile, deep eye sockets but a faintly smiling mouth. Slanting morning light dapples her shoulder like unnamed hope.

A dusty palette by her feet mixes red, yellow, and blue; water stains at the rim remain from last night's spill. She breathes lightly, fingers trembling. The point travels over paper. She keeps revising and erasing the pupils—removing expressions that are “too real,” folds that hold “warmth.”

    LIN RAN (whispering, slightly reproachful): She shouldn't smile. I never draw that kind of smile.

Sound: pencil on paper, breeze through the door gap, distant cat.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Steady approach, slight push into the painted face.
> - Composition: Low camera at eye level, painting fills the left half, her gaze meets the lens.
> - Lighting: Slanting morning, high sidelight, clear warm-cool contrast, slight reflections along shadow edges.
> - Color: Cold gray and gray-blue with pale-gold highlights; clear and chilly.
> - Atmosphere: Distance and introspection; creative instinct coexists with doubt.
> - Music: Slow single piano notes, long breathing spaces.
> - Transition: Enter through door-gap light; push through darkness into the next scene.
> - Narrative function: Establish creative motivation and psychology, planting the central conflict.

### S02: Dinner · INT. · Day ｜ [00:46–1:20]

Lin Ran chops potatoes steadily, mechanically. Opposite, Zhou Ran opens a weekly planner on his phone. “Tomorrow's meeting needs your slide template, timed to the minute.” He glances at her knife work. “I sent your urban women's portraits to a client last week. They said they feel lived-in. But I still think they're not stable enough.”

She stops; a slice falls onto the plate. Without looking at his phone, she wipes flour from her fingertips with a handkerchief. Half a sketch is unconsciously pressed beneath a napkin, its edge creasing.

    ZHOU RAN (calmly): Could we plan our future together? Move to the west side, say. A little child, a little home, but a rhythm to life.

She nods, smile light as a brushstroke. His Family Plan shows a floor plan labeled Children's Room, Kitchen and Dining, Balcony Drying Area. Small text: “Lin Ran, future wife's role: responsible for creating the family atmosphere.” She leans forward, as if listening or calculating.

Sound: chopping, clear drops in a pot, phone taps.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Medium lateral push; close-up of her fingers sliding and the sketch trapped under the napkin corner.
> - Composition: Diagonal table; his phone right, her bowed silhouette left.
> - Lighting: Soft but cool window light, distinct facial shadows.
> - Color: Neutral white, beige table paper, dark-gray clothing; little emotional temperature difference.
> - Atmosphere: Surface harmony, growing inner tension, romance becoming discipline.
> - Music: Piano enters and steadies, with faint water and fan sounds.
> - Transition: Wobbling plate cuts to his phone; rise to the entire table.
> - Narrative function: First interference of love with creation; introduce stability versus freedom.

---

### S03: Evening conversation · INT. · Night ｜ [1:21–2:10]

On the sofa, Lin Ran opens a sketchbook to an old small venue: “Arts Market” on its window, figures moving at the door. Zhou Ran approaches with tea. “You've been painting these last few days. Want to see an exhibition this weekend?”

“An exhibition?” She pauses. “I only paint before exhibitions. Afterward I want to burn it all.”

He chuckles. “That sounds like changing the subject. You say art should be ‘true,’ right? But in real life, we need acceptance.”

She looks through him. “In real life, do we obediently behave, or dare paint truths people dislike?”

Silence. His finger circles the cup's rim twice. “I'm afraid nobody will understand what you paint. You think that's freedom. But loneliness isn't freedom.”

Her fingers shake; the sketchbook slips. Containing panic, she retrieves it. Tea stains the cover and curls a corner. She says nothing, looking outside at a dark city whose lights resemble artificially cut patterns.

Sound: glass lightly meets the table; pages slap open, then rustle.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Follow Zhou Ran, hold the confrontation in medium shot, push into her backlit face.
> - Composition: Frontal view shifts to 45 degrees; she occupies the upper half and looks into the lens.
> - Lighting: Warm yellow room, her face dark in backlight, his clear.
> - Color: Warm yellow against cold gray; her hands dark gray, him warm orange, emotional opposition.
> - Atmosphere: Softness within conflict; cooling words reveal psychological tearing.
> - Music: Stronger piano rhythm with low dissonant chords, heavy and repressed.
> - Transition: Her back cuts to an empty studio; slowly lower to abandoned floor sketches.
> - Narrative function: Direct conflict: love measures being understood, the artist anchors herself in truth.

---

### S04: Passive compromise · INT. · Night ｜ [2:11–3:15]

The studio is now a living room. Zhou Ran's family photographs hang on the wall, including a beaming two-year-old. Their coats occupy a corner beside a model stroller. Her supplies are neatly classified, paint boxes labeled “For Domestic Scenes Only.”

She sits beside the pencil case he reorganized. A note on its edge: “Art may enter everyday life, but must yield.”

Opposite, he watches her leaf through Domestic Scenes: himself cooking, her brushing her teeth nearby, a Family Plan schedule on the wall.

“See? Standardized, this series looks so cozy.” He points, voice flat.

She rubs the paper's edge. “But I'm drawing confusion, not comfort.”

He sighs and sets down his cup. “Neither of us sees everything in black and white. You always want to go alone. I'm afraid you'll go so far nobody can catch you.”

Without speaking, she slowly pushes a pencil to the table's corner. Rain begins against the window, rhythmic as a heartbeat. At the easel, she rolls up an original sketch: a woman hurrying through rain, left shoulder torn open, clutching blank drawing paper. She puts it deep in a drawer.

Sound: growing rain, continuous drops, wind lifting the curtain.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Moving close-ups of supplies, notes, drawings; slow motion for her actions.
> - Composition: Low camera, materials in a horizontal row; her back toward us, body contracting inward.
> - Lighting: Mixed warm interior against cold rain light; dim image.
> - Color: Muted warm yellow and gray-blue, suppressed emotion.
> - Atmosphere: Compromise and repression; familial coexistence conceals a real emotional crisis.
> - Music: Rain dominates; piano disappears, leaving ambient sound and breathing.
> - Transition: Her closing door cuts to the rainy exterior; enlarge the curtain of rain.
> - Narrative function: Love wins, creation is domesticated; foreshadowing lies in “tearing up unfinished sketches.”

---

### S05: Hidden fracture · INT. · Night ｜ [3:16–4:30]

Alone, Lin Ran searches the studio for reference material. In Zhou Ran's notebook she finds a template: “Lin Ran's Future Life.” In a turtleneck, she smiles beside a crib; a child chews an apple, family dinner on the table. Beneath: “Lin Ran's role: gentle, intellectual, able to soothe family emotions.”

Trembling, she turns it over. Handwritten: “If she won't compromise, I'll make her ‘adapt.’”

Voltage outside fluctuates; the light flickers. She seizes the sheet and tears it in two. Paper scatters like a tiny snowfall. Without looking again, she opens fresh paper and draws a woman alone at a nighttime window, city neon behind her, a brush hovering over blankness. An old scar on the right shoulder coincides with Lin Ran's childhood wrist burn.

She concentrates. Her movements become firm; pencil strokes no longer embellish or evade. The camera slowly approaches the scar on the right shoulder, never concealed.

Sound: sharp tearing, faint bulb-current hum, increasingly steady pencil strokes.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push into the notebook, close-up of tearing, quick cut to drawing the new work.
> - Composition: Center Lin Ran, back and side filling the frame, right-shoulder scar facing the lens.
> - Lighting: Cold-white interior, flickering bulb, unstable light intensifying tension.
> - Color: Cold blue, gray-white, metallic blue; faint red at the scar signals eruption.
> - Atmosphere: Anger and awakening; trauma renamed, personal boundaries affirmed.
> - Music: Brief electronic beat changes the rhythm sharply; minimal piano returns with biting tones.
> - Transition: Tear cuts to the new drawing; gently push to the pencil pausing over blank paper.
> - Narrative function: Expose controlling intimacy, resist discipline, awaken the self.

---

### S06: Independent choice · INT. · Day ｜ [4:31–6:00]

Morning fills the restored studio. The easel stands again, paint boxes remain out. A new sketch shows a woman at the city's edge, back to us, clothes flying, brush still raised. The rising sun outside casts gold on her shoulder, a gentle freedom that asks for no help.

Lin Ran brushes the drawing's edge without revising it. She silently picks up another Family Life sketch—Zhou Ran smiling at dinner, herself eating noodles head down—folds it, and puts it in the drawer.

She opens her phone but sends nothing to Zhou Ran. Instead, she creates an Unfinished Projects folder titled “Unnamed Truth.”

By the window she watches a child drawing little figures at the alley entrance. She smiles without asking who the child is or saying “Great drawing.” She simply watches, as though watching a work of her own.

Sound: birds, light leaves, brush on paper, faint running water.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Approach morning light, pull out to the complete painting, pass over sketches in slow motion, cut to her looking at the street.
> - Composition: Medium low-angle shot from behind; skyline extends naturally.
> - Lighting: Warm gold morning, high soft sidelight, no shadows; bright determination.
> - Color: Warm yellow, cream, pale gray; sunlight gold and hope.
> - Atmosphere: Clear and quiet, autonomy and freedom established.
> - Music: Piano returns with an open, lighter melody, rising final notes followed by space.
> - Transition: She looks skyward; hold a long take on the sunlit painting and fade.
> - Narrative function: She actively chooses artistic independence and reconciliation with love rather than compromise, completing the theme.
