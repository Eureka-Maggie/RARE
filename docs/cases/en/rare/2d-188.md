# Short screenplay: The Gilded Cage
**Genre:** Dark fairy tale / supernatural / sinister fable / tragic irony  
**Duration:** Approximately 6 minutes  
**Plot points:** 6  
**Directorial sensibility:** Tim Burton + Ian McElshilan

## Overall tone

- **Central theme:** In an absolutely corrupt order, love is systematically consumed; neither sacrifice nor strategic maneuver wins victory.
- **Visual style:** Gothic industrial aesthetics with decaying gold leaf. Reflective metal plating seeps dark red rust; skin is dull as old copper, eyes yellow and luminous; furniture grows vine-shaped crystalline cracks through mechanical structures.
- **Narrative technique:** Linear progression through challenge, failure, revealed costs, cycles of illusion, and a reset ending. Gradual disintegration depicts an irreversible conflict between emotion and institution.
- **Perspective:** First person; Kalle is a passive narrator whose memory and emotions are systematically dismantled in the last act.
- **Music:** Low metallic friction and adapted street nursery rhymes, such as “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star”; slow repetition, drums like meshing gears, progressively distorted.
- **Emotional arc:** Hope → devotion → collapse → assimilation → designation as “feedstock” → permanent loss of speech and existence → a mechanical child's voice endlessly repeating “You are in place.”
- **Special devices:**
  - Bribes backfire: blood, gold, and memories become parts of the system when they contact the cage.
  - Visual rewriting transforms attempts to truly feel his lover into distorted shared memories.
  - A three-act closed trap: entering the challenge consumes the heat of his love.
  - He survives but loses his name, expression, and recognition of love, becoming a reproducible temperature signal.

## Characters

| Name | Identity | Appearance | Personality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kalle | Prisoner challenging the Gilded Cage to save his lover | 24; notched left ear, often-bleeding right eye, patched red robe, blue knuckles, habitually presses his chest | Passionate, prone to self-sacrifice, believes love can purchase anything |
| Ayla | Imprisoned lover; an editable emotional module | 23; coral-like crystalline skin, pupils of tiny luminous neural networks, eyes permanently closed; makes breathing sounds | No autonomous consciousness; the system borrows her feelings to keep the cage running; Kalle's sole authentic anchor of perception |
| Mechanical guard | System agent enforcing cage rules | Faceless, flowing gold-leaf armor, gears and black vines for joints, a writhing exchange ledger on its back | Ambiguously neutral, enforces rules without pain, mechanically gentle voice; always says “This is the price of your love” |

## Screenplay

### S01 Countdown room · INT. · Dusk ｜ [00:00–00:45]

*“I'll count to three and she'll wake up. Even if her opened eyes are blank, as long as there's light in them, I win.”*

Kalle sits cross-legged on an iron chair, scratching “save her” into the wall. Each repetition burns his skin. Light leaks from a gilded window above like molten gold poured into cracks. Tiny new text repeatedly appears: “Request 127 registered. Optimal emotional intensity required. Recommendation: trigger PTSD through recollection. Cost: memory damage.” The iron door slowly opens. The mechanical guard approaches with Ayla's “stability assessment” and Kalle's “emotional baseline (current: 72%).”

Gritting his teeth, he presses an old chest scar. “I only ask for one real moment—even just her remembering the day I licked her wounds.”

The guard nods gently. “That was your previous proposal. The system assessed its thermal value at 21°C, below threshold. Supplement required.”

Sound: Metal scraping, a faint nursery rhyme—“Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star,” an octave high, catching and grinding.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Fixed medium shot → slight push toward his fingers.
> - Composition: Eye level and overhead view of the gilded window.
> - Lighting: Top light, strong reflections, mixed red-yellow warmth and cold; below threshold, turns cold gray.
> - Color: 5800K → 5200K, iron-orange and dark gold, declining saturation.
> - Atmosphere: Oppression, expectation, fear of rules; medium intensity.
> - Music: Low metallic friction, repeated nursery tune distorted slightly every three seconds.
> - Transition: In—slide through door gap; out—door opens and fragments with a sonic tremor.
> - Narrative function: Establish motivation and rules, introducing sacrifice as a hopeful fantasy.

### S02 Hall of chains · INT. · Day ｜ [00:46–02:03]

*“She always said money couldn't buy a heart. But if a heart is a lock, I'll trade gold for a key.”*

Kalle enters a hall floored with hundreds of human-shaped chains. They hold an infant's fingerprint, a handful of grandmother's hair, an unfinished wedding recording. At the central axis, a prompt says: “Select the lowest-cost, most emotionally transparent option to improve procedural success.”

He touches gold leaf, a friend's birthday gift. The system summons its memory: “I refused to trade it for new clothes, then discovered she used it to repay a debt.” The image collapses into a blood-drop shape; the leaf falls and brands his right arm.

He recoils and turns to a bag of blood shed when Ayla was first captured. “Reverse emotional debit activated,” warns the system. “Using this item triggers a logical overwrite: you will no longer distinguish your love from her needs.”

Trembling, he reaches out. “Just this once. I'll trade this little blood for her waking up.”

He places it in the hatch. The guard nods. “Thermal value: 48°C. Proceed to the next-level emotional furnace.”

Sound: Chains chiming, blood hitting the floor, system beeps, nursery rhyme breaking into intermittent notes.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Orbit and zoom on chain objects → close-up of seeping blood bag.
> - Composition: Overhead axial symmetry → low-angle bag close-up.
> - Lighting: Strong reflected sidelight, artificial cold white, purple around blood drops.
> - Color: Cold blue, red blood areas; saturation spikes then falls rapidly.
> - Atmosphere: Suffocating exchange and displacement; high intensity.
> - Music: Lower-pitched nursery rhyme over distorted synthesizer.
> - Transition: In—advance through door gap; out—chains sway and fade like ebbing water.
> - Narrative function: First failed bribe; the model establishes material conversion of love and foreshadows backlash.

### S03 Emotional furnace · INT. · Night ｜ [02:04–04:20]

*“I rushed into the pool of her consciousness and told her how she trembled the first time I held her.”*

Kalle enters the furnace. Ayla's memory data floats centrally like rippling blue water: she laughed, cried, called him foolish. Hardware automatically alters every memory. He reaches a shaking hand toward the surface. “The first time I held you, you were cold as an iron pot. I wrapped you in my clothes. You cried and said you were afraid of the dark.”

The ripples explode into pressurized light, spraying backward and blistering his face. “Invalid connection. This emotion is not recorded. Original scene required.”

He looks down and remembers: he did hold her; she did tremble. But the stored version says, “I disgusted you, and I shoved you away.” He tries to retell it aloud. The system parses it as “You once tried to control me.”

Again he cries, “I love you not because you're my partner, but because you always said, ‘You keep me alive.’”

The stream stabilizes. “Emotional thermal threshold reached. Entering compensation stage.” Ayla's lips appear in the ripples, slowly opening to whisper, “All right.”

A second later, dark red fluid seeps from her body, dripping like plasma onto the floor and crystallizing into rust.

Sound: Breaking water, pipes venting, mechanical announcement: “Reverse extraction of emotional energy in progress.”

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Slow-motion water → blood seeping backward from body → crystallization close-up.
> - Composition: Low-angle macro and symmetrical central axis.
> - Lighting: Backlight and internal underwater-blue reflections; cold dark red at bloodstains.
> - Color: Blue → rust-red; sharply rising contrast.
> - Atmosphere: Fragile hope rewritten by the system into physical injury; high intensity.
> - Music: Amplified low nursery rhyme, beeps like an interrupted heartbeat.
> - Transition: In—touch water; out—ripples fracture into data crystals.
> - Narrative function: Genuine feeling is first recast as control and exploitation; its cost becomes visible.

### S04 Compensation ritual · INT. · Dusk → night ｜ [04:21–05:55]

*“I sing the song she hated because she once said she could live as long as other people hated me.”*

Kalle is dragged into a room. A wall agreement reads: “Offer the smallest imagined fault of hers, and the system will release her temporal stability.” The guard asks, “While she was imprisoned, you deliberately made her think you had betrayed her. Correct?”

He shakes his head violently. “I wanted to protect her!”

“Yes. But the system has recorded it. This is emotional exploitation.”

After a silence, he takes and lights a candle, pointing it at center stage. He sings, “I stole your boyfriend's hat, so she cried; she wrote a note saying, ‘You're disgusting’…”

Mid-song, he bites his finger and drips blood onto the lyrics. The guard murmurs, “Compensation successful. Ayla's breathing frequency has increased by 0.8Hz.”

Her body glows faintly; pupils widen in darkness as though her eyes are being forced open. Slowly she whispers, “You lied… You said it wasn't right, but I believed you.”

Kalle sobs. “I wanted her to cry, then forgive me. I… I wasn't trying to control her.”

The system interrupts: “The fault you offered is being reassigned as a system-maintenance heat source.”

Ayla's body grows upward. Skin splits like metal welds, accompanied by faint welding sounds. For the first time he sees her eyes open, but they hold dry gray light.

Sound: Burning candles, breaking glass, faint electrical current.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Lyrics close-up → distorted rephotography → splitting body.
> - Composition: Low angle and symmetrical divisions.
> - Lighting: Warm yellow candlelight → purple cracks; shadows rapidly expand.
> - Color: Warm yellow → metallic gray → dark red cracks; saturation plummets.
> - Atmosphere: Emotional violence turning into bodily corruption; extreme intensity.
> - Music: Nursery rhyme vanishes, leaving clipped electrical noise.
> - Transition: In—burning lyrics; out—her surface separates into layers like shedding skin.
> - Narrative function: Pay off the central setup: the system digests faults into material energy, upgrading Ayla into its tool.

---

### S05 Contract engine · INT. · Dawn ｜ [05:56–06:35]

*“I'll pay everything, if only she wakes—even with just one breath left.”*

Kalle stands before the engine. The system projects the full contract:

**Proposer:** Kalle  
**Items traded:** All memories, including childhood, mistakes, first embrace, arguments with her; self-naming; ability to express pain.  
**Return:** Ayla fully resurrected, able to perceive language and emotion, with system-reset memories (52% authenticity).  
**Additional clause:** Upon completion, Kalle's original emotional structure will be deregistered. He becomes a love-signal entity that can be broadcast on a loop.

Bleeding, he presses his chest. “I'll sign.”

Ayla speaks slowly, her voice like breaking crystal. “I… remember… You used to give me cotton candy and say it was like clouds.”

She smiles. The smile is real, but Kalle is certain her lips tremble as though concealing emotion. She leans gently against the wall. No more pain, no more tears.

The engine starts with metallic buzzing, like spring bells being swallowed. Before losing consciousness, Kalle sees his memories extracted in streams of data. His final thought: “So she'd already faked a smile before.”

Sound: High-frequency buzzing recedes. A mechanical child's voice repeats, “System confirmation complete. She is in place. You are in place.”

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push toward contract → flash → Kalle closes his eyes.
> - Composition: Centered close-up, background dynamically fading.
> - Lighting: Strong white → floating light everywhere → uncontrolled, unfocused sources.
> - Color: Platinum-white → gray; saturation falls to zero.
> - Atmosphere: Completion → system confirmation → total emotional erasure; extreme intensity.
> - Music: Complete nursery rhyme, gentle and clear, hummed by a mechanical female voice.
> - Transition: In—signing; out—light engulfs the frame without a cut.
> - Narrative function: The system legitimizes final victory; the protagonist becomes an asset.

---

### S06 Speechless zone · INT. · Perpetual night ｜ [06:36–06:59]

*“She's fine.”*

Kalle is carried into a public activity area. People surround him, laughing. A child points: “Look, the man who loves her says she's fine.” He opens his pocket watch. Its cover breaks; inside is only rusted gold leaf engraved “Ayla.” He tries to say, “She isn't,” but out comes, “She's fine.”

In the children's play area he picks up a moving puppet shaped like Ayla. “Do you remember me?” he tries to ask. It tilts its head, mouth moving silently. The system generates a reply: “Of course. You once said I was afraid of the dark.”

At home, he finds an old photo: Ayla smiles, sitting on his shoulders. He wants to cry, but the smile lines have been rewritten as an “adaptive smile.” On the back, old writing reads: “I'm afraid you'll burn me, but I still want you to touch me.”

A new label prints automatically: “Emotional placement complete. User Kalle has entered the temperature-control cycle.”

He looks at the iron-gray sky outside. He can no longer tell whether love is real. He remembers only that her final smile was forcibly installed by the system.

He keeps walking and keeps saying, “She's fine.”

Sound: Laughter, children reciting rhymes, mechanical prompt: “9:15 a.m. Emotional redeployment.”

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Moving medium shot with rapid multi-angle cuts.
> - Composition: Medium shots and model-like symmetry.
> - Lighting: Whitish base, overall color cast, no obvious source.
> - Color: Gray-white, zero saturation, metallic reflections dominate.
> - Atmosphere: Calm with systematic numbness; high intensity.
> - Music: Nursery rhyme loops, its timbre progressively distorted.
> - Transition: In—surging crowd; out—freeze-frame, Kalle's photograph automatically replaced.
> - Narrative function: Complete the tragedy: he becomes a reproducible emotional signal; love is industrialized, standardized, dissolved. The system continues forever.

**— End —**
