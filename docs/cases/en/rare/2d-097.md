# Short screenplay: The Tree Whisperer
**Genre:** Nature fable / philosophical reflection / drama / court fantasy  
**Duration:** Approximately 6 minutes  
**Plot points:** 6  
**Directorial sensibility:** Hirokazu Kore-eda × the restrained cruelty of Yu Hua's *To Live*

## Overall tone

- **Central theme:** Someone forcibly claims a thousand-year-old mountain tree for its fruit, roots, and spirit, only to discover that ownership is a passing scar. Nature's silent withering and regeneration warn humanity against overstepping its boundaries.
- **Visual style:** Surreal realism. Almost human textures and breathing trees; small physical changes within a quiet forest, from trembling leaf veins to moving roots. Clear moss, earth, and wood textures; cold blue-green tones suggest age and waning life.
- **Narrative technique:** Linear escalation through acts of possession toward an irreversible backlash from nature.
- **Perspective:** Omniscient third person, focused on Lin Ye's attempts to possess the ancient tree; its whispers form a hidden sound thread.
- **Music:** Airy guqin and faint electronic pulses like forest breathing; slow, progressively distorted, moving from peace to fracture.
- **Emotional arc:** Calm → desire → encroachment → terror → dispossession → silent residual warmth → quiet plant regeneration.
- **Special devices:**
  - Branch movements, black sap, and trembling roots externalize the tree's speech as a natural language.
  - After each act of possession, bark cracks grow an inch, intensifying Lin Ye's futility.
  - As a child, he heard, “If the tree does not answer, you are wrong.” Ignored, the line unintentionally returns at the end.
  - Stillness opposes movement: the more frantic the human action, the slower yet more complete nature's response.

## Screenplay

### S01 Meeting at the mountain pass · INT./EXT. · Dusk ｜ [00:00–00:38]

*“It has been waiting for me. Waiting for me to claim it.”*

Lin Ye stops his old motorcycle before the tree. The headlight sweeps over bark like light entering a tomb. The trunk is roughly ten meters tall; its top cannot be seen directly, only cloudlike interwoven branches and rustling leaves. Seven deep grooves mark it like handprints. He slowly dismounts, takes a small bag of seeds from his backpack, and presses them into a crack between the roots. His knuckles tremble as he strokes the bark. Awe quickly becomes excitement. The bark suddenly warms. Bloodlike drops seep from the trunk's fissures. He catches them and pours them into a glass bottle; its walls darken.

    LIN YE (low, almost to himself): “The elders said no one could claim the mountain. I called it superstition. But it's responding now, isn't it?”

Sound: Wind in leaves, distant birds, motorcycle engine stopping, metal frame rubbing.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Close shot to medium tracking shot; slow push toward bark cracks.
> - Composition: Low angle; tree fills 70%, Lin Ye in the lower right.
> - Lighting: Side backlight; cold yellow overlaps blue, bark reflects green; contrast soft as passing wind.
> - Color: 6500K, low saturation, blue-gray and pale green; tentative desire.
> - Atmosphere: Quiet with a small breathing pulse; medium intensity.
> - Music: Sustained guqin notes like echoes in an empty valley; steady rhythm.
> - Transition: In—blurred fade-in; out—wind carries the cut.
> - Narrative function: Establish his misjudgment that nature can be understood and controlled through action.

### S02 Carving a name, picking fruit · INT./EXT. · Noon ｜ [00:39–01:22]

*“I have to prove I own it.”*

With an iron-handled knife, Lin Ye carves “Third generation of the Lin family” into the south side of the trunk. The blade draws tiny cracks like joints opening and closing. He picks the first fruiting branch. Its amber skin holds points of light. He immerses the fruit in a cup and studies its veins: a faint pulse, like a heart. Excited, he pats the trunk. “From now on, I'll raise it and make it bear fruit.” He pulls tissue paper from his pocket and gently covers the earth at its roots, as though planting flowers.

The roots twitch. A low hum rises from the soil. The fruit liquid suddenly clouds, black wisps floating up. He does not notice. He keeps carving: “Returns in three years.” Finished, he gasps, as if exhaling after torture. He hides the fruit in a wooden box and shoulders his pickaxe to leave.

    LIN YE (panting, forceful): “It says I'm the owner. Then it should bear treasure.”

Sound: Knife carving, liquid moving, bark vibrating, distant pecking birds.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Close-up of carving → medium shot of fruit details → slow pullback.
> - Composition: Frontal eye level; figure centered against the tree, cracks clearly visible.
> - Lighting: Harsh noon sun and deep bark shadows; strong contrast makes possession painfully glaring.
> - Color: 5600K, high saturation, amber and dark brown; swelling possessiveness.
> - Atmosphere: Agitation and lost control; high intensity.
> - Music: Faster guqin with electronic drums; an aggressive rhythm.
> - Transition: In—fade with knife sound; out—cut to shaking fruit liquid.
> - Narrative function: First act of possession; the tree's response becomes a reaction to invasion.

### S03 A cage for the roots · INT./EXT. · Night ｜ [01:23–02:45]

*“It can run. I have to lock it up.”*

Late at night, Lin Ye takes a shovel and steel mesh three meters beneath the roots. He wraps three main roots in wire mesh, secures it with wooden stakes, and drives nails into the bark with a creak. Hammering the roots, he says, “You cannot move. You belong to me.” The roots shake violently. Soil caves in, deep fissures opening like raised wounds. The wind stops. Lin Ye shouts, “Did you hear me? You can't move anymore!” The roots jerk three times. An inch-long crack spreads upward from underground, seeping thick black liquid into the soil.

He lights a torch and finds tiny glowing insects crawling over the roots. Frantically he swats; the swarm scatters and black fluid slides out like blood. He swallows a piece of earth and mutters, “The old rule says no one can claim the mountain. But this tree is a dead thing. If I take everything, it ought to die.” He reinforces the mesh and chains, convinced that “it can never be taken away again.”

Sound: Hammer blows, collapsing soil, sliding black fluid, faint electronic humming.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Overhead roots → macro drops of black liquid → slow retreat.
> - Composition: High angle; roots occupy 60%, Lin Ye in front; strongly oppressive.
> - Lighting: Sidelight; cold reflections on mesh, roots pulsing in shadow; high contrast.
> - Color: Cool 4800K, black-gray and fluorescent green; fear intertwined with fanaticism.
> - Atmosphere: Oppression, unease, physical discomfort; high intensity.
> - Music: Electronic tones change pitch and distort, like life being cut apart.
> - Transition: In—shaking subsides; out—black liquid begins to drip.
> - Narrative function: First failure of “ultimate possession”; the tree starts systematic resistance, spreading cracks as natural symptoms.

### S04 Speech broken by rain · INT./EXT. · Stormy night ｜ [02:46–04:05]

*“It's breaking. I can't watch it break.”*

Rain pours down. Determined to keep the roots dry, Lin Ye drives two stakes into the south side to reinforce the mesh. Rain washes the bark; cracks rapidly widen. Roots click like breaking joints. He climbs up to pull the mesh and discovers an uprooted tendril. Roots extend through the mud as if struggling. “Tree! Whose are you?!” he shouts. The trunk convulses. Leaves droop together as though torn by wind; their edges split and dark red liquid seeps to the ground.

He rushes indoors, boiling water in an attempt to heat the body, but finds new roots extending into the air from a drainage hole at the tree's base. “You won't survive! I'll seal you shut!” He strikes the mesh. It rebounds into the bark, widening the split with a groaning moan.

He scrambles back and slaps the trunk. A new inscription has appeared: “Thou shalt return; thou shalt rest.” Water blurs the letters like tears. His hands shake as he tears open the wooden box to retrieve the fruit. It is cracked, black, and withered.

Sound: Torrential rain, tearing roots, groaning trunk, drops striking leaves, breaking mesh.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Handheld shake → close-up of widening split → falling leaves → push toward sap.
> - Composition: Medium low-angle shot; tree above center, emphasizing collapse.
> - Lighting: Backlight through rain and mist; sidelight casts shadows at the split, light patches like wounds.
> - Color: 4000K, low saturation, iron-gray and dark red; breakdown.
> - Atmosphere: Despair and dispossession; extreme intensity.
> - Music: Guqin cuts out; electronic sound fragments and decays into static.
> - Transition: In—storm bursts in; out—dripping continues.
> - Narrative function: Central reversal: possession provokes nature's backlash and mental collapse; the tree suggests its owner must learn to breathe with nature.

### S05 The stolen spirit · INT./EXT. · Next morning ｜ [04:06–05:10]

Lin Ye wakes before dawn. Inside the house, the mesh at his feet is badly rusted, nails loosened, roots covered in moss and new shoots. He reaches for the fruit in the iron box, but it has long since cracked and dried. His fingertips sting at its touch. He sneers, “Just a poisonous fruit, isn't it? I should have thrown it away.” He curses the tree: “You hurt me. You shouldn't have lived this long!”

After washing, he finds the words “Thou shalt return; thou shalt rest” clearly legible. A new pattern winds like a snake across the bark. He touches it; the bark trembles and gives off faint light. Kneeling, he wants to cry but feels only a dry throat. He presses his face to the trunk. A tiny “beep…” sounds like breathing, echoing through an empty valley. He recoils, trembling.

Searching a box for old documents, he finds his father's notebook: “No one may claim the mountain. Trees know. Their silence means they will not let you understand.” He drops to the floor, tears the notebook up, and throws it into the stove. After the fire dies, drifting ash faintly spells out: “You swallow its blood; it spits out your soul.”

Sound: Morning wind, rust scraping, tearing paper, a faint hum echoing near the roots.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Medium push toward bark → close-up of spreading patterns → slow Lin Ye's kneeling movement.
> - Composition: Eye level; tree and human suggest being overtaken by nature.
> - Lighting: Slanting morning light, soft bark reflections, golden crack edges signal repair.
> - Color: 5800K, medium saturation, light green and gray-white; collapse of understanding.
> - Atmosphere: Pain and emptiness; high intensity.
> - Music: Scattered guqin notes like remnants in wind; a subtle tonal change.
> - Transition: In—silent confrontation; out—falling paper ash.
> - Narrative function: Reveal possession's cost: the tree's return means the human spirit's withdrawal; earlier foreshadowing pays off.

---

### S06 Tree language reborn · INT./EXT. · Late winter / early spring ｜ [05:11–06:00]

Lin Ye sits before the tree without touching it. He removes his iron wrist guards and lays them at a crack in the roots, watching shoots emerge, green as newborn down. Wind sways the crown, leaves quivering like breath. He only watches. Three days pass. Cracks heal by themselves, the bark turns green, and faint light pulses. Softly he says, “I'll never carve you again.”

As wind passes, new language slowly emerges on the trunk—not speech, but something carried by wind. In the spring breeze, tiny vibrations reach his fingertips like a whisper:

“You forced me to breathe, and I hurt. You wanted to possess me, and I sprouted. You let go, and I grew.”

Lin Ye closes his eyes in silence. He leaves, throws the old mesh into a mountain gully, and leaves only a little ash. New branches grow from the roots toward the sky, crooked but resolute. No fruit, no carvings. Only stillness.

Sound: Wind, trembling leaf veins, a distant stream. No spoken dialogue; environmental wind grows louder.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Static long take → slow push toward shoots → wide view of the opening canopy.
> - Composition: Medium low angle; tree overwhelmingly dominant, human tiny.
> - Lighting: Warm morning daylight, low contrast, tree shadows stretching like human forms.
> - Color: 5600K, high saturation, tender green and orange-yellow; lingering resonance.
> - Atmosphere: Calm, renewal, repair; gentle intensity.
> - Music: Guqin returns, unhurried, blending with wind in a complete cycle.
> - Transition: In—silent transition; out—wind recedes.
> - Narrative function: Resolve the theme: humans should respect coexistence rather than possess; nature wins silently, leaving a long resonance.

**— End —**
