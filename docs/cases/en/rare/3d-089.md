# Short screenplay: Qingzhan in the Old House
**Genre**: Fantasy / Prejudice / Growth / Family  
**Duration**: Approximately 7 minutes  
**Plot points**: 6  
**Directorial sensibility**: Hayao Miyazaki's gentle fantasy, with the agency of a marginal character in *A Little Monster's Summer*

---

## Overall tone
- **Central idea**: Prejudice is like the bricks of an old house, silent and hard. Only being seen and heard can slowly weather it into light.
- **Visual style**: A traditional Chinese house amid mountain mist; elaborate architectural details within a solid structure. The spirit is half human, half object: furry limbs, a greenish skin-coat, glassy eyes. The texture falls between ink wash and meticulous gongbi painting.
- **Narrative approach**: Linear, from an observer's position. Everyday details and one decisive contact redefine the “monster.”
- **Point of view**: Limited third person, focused on Grandma Chen and Qingzhan, gradually descending into the spirit's perceptions.
- **Music**: Low guqin and wind chimes, slow beats, occasional bamboo flute. Distance gives way to warmth; the ending is light as dew.
- **Emotional arc**: Vigilance → rejection → strange observation → unexpected empathy → resistance and mutual help → gentle acceptance.
- **Devices**:
  1. **Recurring motif**: Qingzhan drips water beneath the eaves each night, like tears, forming a visual memory.
  2. **Sound metaphor**: His “voice” is variously mistaken for crying, a strange call, or wind, then identified as running water.
  3. **Spatial symbolism**: The house's nested rooms, sheltering and being sheltered, parallel the way people come to understand an other.
  4. **Reversed identity**: A watched foreign object becomes a protective spirit.

## Characters
| Name | Identity | Appearance | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grandma Chen | Widowed guardian of the house, 60 | Stooped; gray-blue cloth clothes; frost-white hair; raised veins on her hands; bamboo cane | Habitually wary, deeply prejudiced against outsiders; dismisses instead of understanding |
| Qingzhan | A spirit returned from the mountains, about 7; half human, half wood | Child's body, greenish skin, dry-vine hair; a small blue-green lamp at his waist brightens and dims with his feelings | Quiet, slow but precise; expresses himself through natural sounds; hides in corners |
| Dahua | Neighbor's daughter, 13, a frequent visitor | Red dress, green headscarf; likes drawing spirits and respects boundaries | Briefly encounters Qingzhan; first to treat him without othering him, shifting the atmosphere |

## Screenplay

### S01: First meeting · INT./EXT. · Day ｜ [00:00–00:18]

*“This house is for people, not ‘things.’”*

Moss grows on the front steps; mountain mist drifts beyond the bamboo fence. Grandma Chen carries an iron basin across the blue-gray bricks, moving stiffly. Beside her feet, a small figure in green curls in a corner. He has no face, yet two small eyes watch her, deep as pools, neither hiding nor flinching. An unlit blue-green lamp at his waist sways faintly, as if breathing.

Grandma Chen stops and overturns the basin on the ground. Coldly: “Hey! Which mountain did this wild thing crawl down from? Get off my land!”

The child stays still, drawing the lamp inward like folded wings. The basin lands with a dull clang; water splashes. Wind passes. One clear drop falls from the eaves, exactly in rhythm with the swaying lamp.

Sound: falling iron basin, splashing water, clear eave drips regular as a heartbeat.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: A stable long take pushes from Grandma Chen's back to the corner, then cuts to the child's profile. No shake.
> - Composition: Low camera emphasizes ground-to-figure proportions. Qingzhan is pressed to the frame's edge.
> - Lighting: Natural side-backlight on mottled walls; deep shadow around Qingzhan, faint light only near the lamp, splitting darkness and light.
> - Color: Cool blue-gray, gray-blue sky; the child's green clothes and lamp seem conspicuous.
> - Atmosphere: Distance and vigilance; quiet enough to hear the brick joints.
> - Music: Slow single-note guqin slides, notes nailed into the ground, without emotional variation.
> - Transition: Enter from a street-corner wide shot with the basin's impact. Pull back as the drips continue, flowing onward without a cut.
> - Narrative function: Establish prejudice and the conflict between an outsider and a home.

### S02: Rejection · Main hall · Day ｜ [00:19–00:42]

*“Monsters should not be inside the house.”*

Grandma Chen kneels before a deity. Incense smoke rises; ash falls on the bricks. She mutters: “The mountain god takes them. People or spirits, the rules are all that matter.”

Qingzhan curls below the hall steps, green clothes against the ground, head down. He only looks up once, calmly. Flickering incense light outlines his feather-like hair: a few fine green vines hang from his shoulders, trembling like little branches.

Grandma Chen looks up sharply. “Still swaying there? Last night I heard you calling in the hole in the wall. Were you going to eat people?!”

Qingzhan slowly raises a hand and gently pushes a wooden joint at the corner. A narrow opening reveals a strand of dark green moss. He says nothing. The lamp glows at his fingertips; woven green light seeps from the crack and brushes her shoe.

She jumps back, overturning a bamboo stool with a crash. “You little bastard! How dare you touch my house!” She lunges to grab him.

Qingzhan shrinks away. The lamp goes out, leaving a dim gray-green ember. He retreats into the hall's corner, curled tight, hair hanging like wind-broken grass.

Sound: crackling incense, breaking stool, Grandma Chen's rapid breathing, Qingzhan's weak breaths, the lamp's almost inaudible puff.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Medium shot follows Grandma Chen; quick cut to contact with Qingzhan. Rapid push-in and withdrawal express fear.
> - Composition: Symmetrical, Grandma Chen central, Qingzhan at lower right; altar and walls constrict the space.
> - Lighting: Central warm candlelight catches her profile. Qingzhan hides in shadow, with only the lamp's illusory glow; strong contrast.
> - Color: Warm yellow against his blue-gray zone, swallowed by black when the lamp dies.
> - Atmosphere: Violence, fear, oppressive authority. Her anger feels live-action; his silence contrasts with it.
> - Music: Taut, rapid guqin; drums synchronize with her shouting.
> - Transition: Cut from courtyard to hall with an opening fan and incense catching. After he curls up, withdraw behind the door to a faint line of light.
> - Narrative function: Make prejudice concrete: the spirit signifies danger, and the relationship becomes antagonistic.

### S03: Mishearing · West wing · Dusk ｜ [00:43–01:15]

*“It's crying… It's crying!”*

Wind stirs the bamboo blind. Dahua leans in with a red bowl. “Grandma, I made a new drawing. There's a little spirit, and I drew it dripping water under the eaves.”

Grandma Chen frowns. “What spirit? I don't keep spirits here.”

Dahua smiles. “That little green thing downstairs? It keeps ‘calling’ beneath the eaves at night. I heard it. Like crying.”

Grandma Chen scoffs. “You heard wrong. Wind under the eaves. Just dripping water.”

Yet she remains still, glancing toward the roof corner. Qingzhan bends over, looking down beneath the eaves. His joined hands gently press the edge of a fallen stone, as though supporting something. The lamp trembles in his palms, making green shadows dance at his fingertips.

A gust; one drop splashes on stone. Qingzhan contracts, his lamp flashes and relights. The pattern repeats: falling water → one drop → lamp on → his hand lowers → the next drop.

Dahua holds her breath. “He's… he's not ‘calling.’ He's… waiting for the water to fall?”

Grandma Chen glares. “Shut up! You don't understand anything!” She turns and walks away. Dahua watches the eaves. Softly: “He's waiting. As if someone will come back.”

Sound: wind in the blind, clear regular drips, Dahua's sigh, Grandma Chen's footsteps, a tiny click as Qingzhan touches stone.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Begin from Dahua's viewpoint, slowly circle the house and stop at the eaves, emphasizing the rhythm of Qingzhan and the drops.
> - Composition: Oblique medium shot, Dahua left, Qingzhan right; soft-focus droplets trace a moving path.
> - Lighting: Golden sunset through the west window; Qingzhan at the backlit edge, outlined only by his lamp. Lamp and droplets mirror each other.
> - Color: Warm gold, with Qingzhan still blue-gray; faint green lamplight provides an inner contrast.
> - Atmosphere: A subtle change. Prejudice wavers as wind and water acquire rhythm and order.
> - Music: Sustained guqin notes, joined by high bamboo-flute slides that answer the drops rather than oppose them.
> - Transition: Cut from outside the hall to the wind-lifted blind. Light fades, Grandma Chen recedes, and drips continue.
> - Narrative function: Establish dripping as Qingzhan's expression, misread as crying, preparing for understanding.

### S04: Snowy night · Back courtyard · Night ｜ [01:16–02:30]

*“It fears the cold. It fears being seen.”*

A snowstorm. Ice on the eaves. Qingzhan curls in a snowbank, his lamp faint beneath him, like a firefly. He shivers; hair sticks to his face like frozen grass.

Grandma Chen emerges with an oil lamp. “What are you hiding here for?! Snow's up to your waist! Get out!”

He does not move. Head lowered, he holds a lamp blinking slowly as a heartbeat. Windblown snow makes it flicker. She advances with an iron rod, prying the snow aside to drag him out.

Qingzhan raises a hand, touching the rod with a fingertip. His lamp flares like a star, revealing a small blue-jade bell buried in the snow, engraved with the character “Ying.”

Grandma Chen recoils, hand shaking. “You're… hiding things?!”

He quietly returns the lamp to his waist and closes his eyes. Snow falls harder. The lamp dies; he curls up, hair drooping like flattened reeds.

She stares, stops pressing him, and crouches instead. Softly: “Aren't you… afraid of the cold?”

He opens watery eyes. His voice is barely a whisper in the wind: “Afraid.”

She freezes. His first word to her is neither a call nor a cry. It is “afraid.”

After a long silence, she reaches out, gently resting her hand on his shoulder instead of grabbing. He trembles. The lamp relights, weak as before but steady as breathing.

Snow falls silently. The lamp at his shoulder slowly falls into rhythm with her hand.

Sound: howling snowstorm, rod creaking through snow, lamp's hum, Qingzhan's breath-like whisper, Grandma Chen's slowing breath, soft snowfall.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: High overhead wide shot of the snow; slowly approach the confrontation, ending in close-up on hand, shoulder, and relit lamp.
> - Composition: Low angle places Grandma Chen below Qingzhan; stretched proportions express power and frailty. Contact balances the frame.
> - Lighting: White snow, warm indoor yellow, cold cyan around Qingzhan; his lamp divides the light into intersecting green and yellow.
> - Color: Snow white and cold blue, with green hair and lamp: warmth inside cold.
> - Atmosphere: The darkest point, followed by silent empathy and tenderness in the chill.
> - Music: Guqin briefly stops. Flute enters one note at a time, answering “afraid” and the light.
> - Transition: Cut from outside the west wing into wind and snow. Once the lamp lights, rise to the house's snowy silhouette, then cut to the lightwell.
> - Narrative function: Break through prejudice. Qingzhan's inner feeling is seen; Grandma Chen empathizes, and acceptance begins.

---

### S05: Sleeping together · Lightwell · Night ｜ [02:31–03:45]

*“It is not a monster. It simply was never allowed to sleep.”*

Late night. Grandma Chen invites Qingzhan into the lightwell, lays a straw mat, and sets the lamp on it. “No more hiding in corners tonight. When you sleep, you can see the wind.”

He sits slowly, hand on the lamp, as if awaiting judgment. She gently adjusts his hair. “You're so small the wind could knock you over. I'll get you a quilt.”

From the bottom of a chest she takes an old cotton quilt, blue-dyed cloth worn at the edges. He watches, eyes shining. She covers him so gently she seems afraid to startle him.

The light steadies beneath the quilt. His breathing eases into a child's light sleep. She sits beside him, eyes closed, resting without a word.

At some point he turns and lightly touches the back of her foot, as though stroking grass. She starts, half opens her eyes, but stays still, watching the lamp.

It glimmers blue, like a firefly. She suddenly smiles. “You touched my foot… like I touched your grandmother's when I was little.”

He stays still, but the lamp brightens as if answering. Wind outside; one drop falls from the eaves, and the lamp sways with it.

Grandma Chen touches that drop. Then, for the first time, she lifts his lamp high beside the window. Its shadow lengthens like wings.

“Look,” she whispers. “It's flying for you now.”

Qingzhan says nothing. The lamp rocks in the wind like wings.

Sound: wind, steady drips, faint lamp movement, her low warm laugh, the almost inaudible brush of his hand against her foot, like sliding grass.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: A steady medium shot slowly moves from the entire lightwell to the pair, holding on the lamp shadow and touch.
> - Composition: Symmetrical, at eye level, the two sit side by side. The raised lamp creates a motif of flight.
> - Lighting: Slanting light enters from above; the small lamp creates a pocket of protection, casting shadows to the eaves.
> - Color: Warm orange with cyan-blue lamplight; cool and warm coexist.
> - Atmosphere: Softness, inclusion, emerging trust. Concrete companionship dissolves prejudice.
> - Music: Minimal guqin and a sustained flute note breathe in time with the lamp.
> - Transition: Cut from snow to the lightwell as snow and wind stop and moonlight enters. Follow the lengthening shadow up to the roof.
> - Narrative function: Qingzhan changes from expelled outsider to accepted companion; Grandma Chen's understanding changes.

---

### S06: Remaining warmth · Lightwell · Early morning ｜ [03:46–04:00]

*“It still drips, just as before.”*

First light brightens the blue-gray bricks. Grandma Chen stands beneath the eaves, holding Qingzhan's lamp and gently sweeping it along the roof edge.

He sits motionless beside the stone trough. The lamp remains dimly lit, like something newborn. He looks up at her, eyes bright and calm.

She returns the lamp to his waist. “Today, you don't have to hide.”

He nods slightly; the lamp brightens with the movement. One drop falls from the eaves, clear and regular, exactly as yesterday and the night before.

She smiles and walks back into the house, receding through the morning light.

The camera withdraws. Water forms lines beneath the roof; several drops fall together. The lamp's faint light sways in the wind, like wings, like tears.

Wind stirs bamboo shadows. New leaves rustle.

Sound: morning birds, wind in bamboo, one clear repeated drop.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Long tracking shot follows Grandma Chen while Qingzhan remains seated. End on the whole lightwell, rows of drops marking time.
> - Composition: Overhead pan stresses the continuity of water and lamp. Qingzhan stays at the edge, part of nature.
> - Lighting: Golden morning and bright eaves; cool cyan water and lamp silently face one another across time.
> - Color: Pale yellow and green blend warmly; Qingzhan is unobtrusive but recognizable.
> - Atmosphere: Gentle reconciliation and lingering feeling, conveyed only by silence and dripping.
> - Music: A final high guqin note slides away like cooking smoke; flute quietly closes, resonance lingering.
> - Transition: No transition; naturally fade out while the dripping continues.
> - Narrative function: Prejudice has dissolved. Acceptance is everyday life. Qingzhan is no longer merely seen, but allowed to exist.
