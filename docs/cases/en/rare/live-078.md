# Short screenplay: A Morning of Wandering Thoughts
**Genre**: Realism / Black comedy / Mood piece / Everyday observation  
**Duration**: Approximately 6 minutes  
**Plot points**: 6  
**Directorial sensibility**: Hirokazu Kore-eda × Apicha Borcharin × Peter Chan: realistic texture, emotional glimmers, light-comedy structure

---

## Overall tone
- **Central idea**: In ordinary life's smallest gaps, people fill emptiness with absurd thinking. Invisible wandering thoughts reveal suppressed loneliness and tenderness.
- **Visual style**: Minimal domestic spaces, macro details, slightly shallow focus, desaturated everyday colors.
- **Narrative approach**: Linear everyday actions carry leaping thoughts underneath; displaced actions and absurd logic create comic contrast.
- **Point of view**: First-person interior centered on Lin Xiaoman, faithfully showing fragmented thinking.
- **Music**: Low electronic pulses, spatial silence, occasional discordant snippets such as children's piano or light jazz drums; slow, matching thought.
- **Emotional arc**: Calm → absurd episode → inner wavering → self-mockery → emotional crack → gentle close.
- **Devices**: Repeated leaps such as “I'll lose my job today because yesterday I cooked rice grains into stars” create humor and rhythm. Inner monologue contradicts background action, as when washing hands while wondering whether a cat saw her. Toothbrush, rice cooker, and fridge magnet make thoughts tangible and carry feeling.

## Characters

## Screenplay

### S01: Brushing teeth · INT. · Day ｜ [00:00–00:45]

*“When I brush my teeth, I always think the toothpaste means the opposite. It wants me to disappear, not turn white.”*

Lin Xiaoman runs cold water over her palms and quickly applies toothpaste. She brushes forcefully along her gums, as if breaking a seal. Suddenly she looks into the mirror and hums a half-remembered southern folk song.

Head down, teeth clenched, she brushes faster. Her eyes fix. “I'm quitting today because last night I dreamed ants carried my payslip into the rain to dance.”

She freezes, clothes still trembling, then composes herself, straightens, and flings the toothbrush into the sink. Water splashes. Looking down, she frowns. “No. I didn't dream yesterday. I just knocked over the rice paste.”

Sound: drops, brushing, soft headphone-like background sound of distant children humming.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Close-up, closer push, sudden pullback to full body, return to close-up.
> - Composition: Overhead brush and gums, eye-level mirrored face, side view of her back leaving.
> - Lighting: Natural cold-white sidelight, high contrast; mirror details create slight distance.
> - Color: Desaturated cold blue, faint-yellow toothpaste, gray-blue water; fatigue.
> - Atmosphere: Sluggish absurdity and self-doubt, mild anxiety suppressed by routine.
> - Music: Extremely faint heartbeat-like electronic pulse synchronized with brushing.
> - Transition: Pan from toothbrush to kitchen handle; it opens, cutting to the kitchen.
> - Narrative function: Establish her thinking, first embody absurd daily life, foreshadow the rice-paste incident.

### S02: Breakfast porridge · INT. · Day ｜ [00:46–1:20]

*“If rice grains are stardust, cooking porridge is sowing the universe.”*

She pours rice into a glass pot, covers it, lights the gas. Staring blankly, she presses the lid's edge as if checking for cosmic microwave background radiation.

Stirring gently, she stops. “The grains are moving. They're holding a meeting about whether to become fish.”

She moves the old “Don't Give Up” magnet from the stove's right side to the pot, as if ceremonially protecting it.

“They say if I don't give up today, I'll become tidal energy.” She stirs more slowly. Steam clouds the lid's edge.

Her phone reads “Cellular network enabled.” She jerks it down. “The system says the network's back. But my personal antenna broke long ago.”

Sound: gas flame, soft steam, gentle phone buzz.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Stable medium pan, macro moving rice, quick phone cut, return to pot.
> - Composition: Push-pull arrangement, grains central focus, magnet naturally centered.
> - Lighting: Warm yellow, steam-lit pot rim forms a hot-colored ring.
> - Color: Warm yellow, cream rice, golden lid edge; illusion of a hot porridge pot.
> - Atmosphere: Absurdity and self-packaged loneliness, tender satire.
> - Music: Slow light piano with a repeating bar, like a variation on *Half Past Two at Midnight*.
> - Transition: Enter on rising steam; wind disperses it, pull out toward the living-room floor.
> - Narrative function: Deepen thought projection; cooking carries fantasy, foreshadowing spilled rice paste.

---

### S03: Changing clothes · INT. · Day ｜ [1:21–2:05]

*“My wardrobe isn't like my life. It's orderly. But I left ‘me’ in the second drawer.”*

She faces four drawers in a living-room corner: T-shirts above, old socks midway, a Discarded folder at the bottom. From the third drawer she takes a navy coat, hesitates, replaces it.

In the second, a note: “My life has been reduced a dimension. Only three seconds left to undo.” She tears it off and throws it away, pauses for two seconds, then retrieves it.

She loops a wool scarf around her left hand and rubs it as if checking temperature. “My boyfriend gave me this last year. He said he dreamed I welded the roof with it.”

She freezes, then looks at an old key in the corner. “I really did lose the key once. But that day I didn't get the car fixed. I sold it for a milk tea.”

She returns the key, averts her eyes, mechanically searches for fresh clothes.

Sound: sliding drawers, tearing paper, wool friction, faint draft through the door.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Pan, macro note, quick key cut, repeated pullbacks, return to drawer.
> - Composition: Low approach emphasizes stacked order; she remains marginal.
> - Lighting: Natural diagonal light from a wall corner; uneven floor light, clear drawer-edge silhouettes.
> - Color: Gray-white, old beige drawers, jarring fluorescent-orange note.
> - Atmosphere: Oppression, daily helplessness, fractured logic exquisitely controlled.
> - Music: Small electronic interruptions every 5 seconds, synchronized with heartbeat.
> - Transition: Enter as she touches the wardrobe edge; draft through the gap cuts to drips beneath the kitchen sink.
> - Narrative function: Show thought-life displacement and self-deception, strengthen routine's cycle.

---

### S04: Washing dishes · INT. · Day ｜ [2:06–3:10]

*“Every time I wash dishes, it's like washing a deleted video.”*

She turns on the sink and wipes a glass with old cotton. “A glass is a meditation vessel. Washing it soaks yesterday's feelings out.”

Seeing a crack in its base, she stares. “That crack looks exactly like last week on the subway, when a child fell and I didn't dare help.”

She replaces the glass, slowly scrubs the sink edge with a toothbrush as though cleaning a symbol. Bending to adjust the water, she hears a drop thud into the sink. She looks up and makes an intensely serious, almost ritual gesture.

“Today I'm pouring away the world.”

She pours water down the drain. The flow turns black, instantly dispersed by a newly switched-on fan. She exhales, sits, eyes slightly red.

Her phone says 10:18 a.m. She opens an old video: “My friend says, ‘You always shut yourself away when the weather's good.’”

Sound: drops, cloth friction, fan starting, video-loading notification.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Medium shot, macro crack, hand close-up, transition to old video on screen.
> - Composition: Knee-level low view, symmetrical glass and sink, sudden rise to her face.
> - Lighting: Cold white, distinct underwater shadows, cold flow contrast, faint darkness in the crack.
> - Color: Cold gray, black water, dark red at the crack; oppressive and visually dirty but structurally clear.
> - Atmosphere: Heartache, repression, self-punishing release; real vulnerability through absurdity.
> - Music: Score stops; only water and low fan, then an extremely faint child's hummed phrase.
> - Transition: Dispersing water leads to a kitchen-wide view; cut to the windblown quilt on the balcony.
> - Narrative function: First emotional overflow; fantasy becomes reflection, triggering the central heartache.

---

### S05: Airing the quilt · INT. · Afternoon ｜ [3:11–4:20]

Chen Abao sits on a balcony stool, reading and softly coughing. Lin Xiaoman slowly hangs a quilt on the line.

Watching it sway, she says: “It's free now. It's feeling the wind's body temperature.”

He looks up, mouth slightly open, says nothing, puts down his paper and blows on his tea.

She continues: “Yesterday I dreamed I became a cat. But the cat said it didn't know me.”

He slowly looks up. “Watching you hang that quilt, you'd think you were atoning.”

She freezes, turns, studies her old slippers. “I never knew other people could see my feelings too.”

She pulls the quilt taut, starts to take it down, then stops and gently strokes a corner, as if checking whether a memory remains whole.

Sound: wind, moving quilt, his light cough, puffs of steam from tea.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Wide to medium, fixed shot of Chen Abao, close-up of her hands.
> - Composition: Diagonal, her left, him right, tension between their gazes.
> - Lighting: Warm afternoon yellow; quilt casts moving shadows displaced from human ones.
> - Color: Warm yellow, brown quilt, dark-green cup; bright without warmth.
> - Atmosphere: Quiet, subtle resonance; tender understanding inside sadness.
> - Music: Soft jazz drums, repeated single beats, enter slowly without disturbing speech.
> - Transition: Wind lifts quilt, camera tilts up; cut to doorbell.
> - Narrative function: First outside person offers possible empathy, establishing an emotional current between people.

---

### S06: Doorbell · INT. · Day ｜ [4:21–5:50]

Returning from the balcony, she hears the doorbell and hesitates by the door, holding the old scarf.

Two rings. She opens. Chen Abao holds a bag of hot porridge.

“Just made it. Heard you were having a rough day, so I prepared this.”

She blushes and wants to hide, but takes it. Looking down, stirring: “I've wondered so many times what animal I'd become if I didn't go to work today.”

He smiles and lifts his cup. “Two answers for you: a talking fish, or a plastic bag in a typhoon.”

She laughs, eyes slightly red, handing the porridge back. “Actually… I wish I could be paper blown away.”

He nods and sets the porridge by her door. “Then be the wind.”

She watches his back and slowly closes the door. In the kitchen she looks at the uncooked rice. Softly: “The rice is still waiting for me.”

Sound: doorbell, opening and closing door, light porridge-container contact, his quiet laugh, rising wind.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Exterior medium shot, close exchange of looks, interior porridge close-up, kitchen interior.
> - Composition: Face-on exchange, side view of closing door, return to a kitchen-corner angle.
> - Lighting: Warm natural light through the gap, his back silhouetted behind the door.
> - Color: Warm orange, amber porridge, cream paper in wind symbolizes being carried away.
> - Atmosphere: Lingering feeling; absurdity and reality merge, sadness turns to tender release.
> - Music: Score fades into the background, leaving wind and the distant city's low hum.
> - Transition: Enter on a kitchen mirror reflection; fade to black, hold 3 seconds, text appears: “We all have wandering thoughts.”
> - Narrative function: Close the emotional arc: wandering thoughts express loneliness but can also be understood and warmed.
