# Short screenplay: Beyond the Canvas
**Genre**: Relationships / Art / Women's growth / Introspection  
**Duration**: Approximately 6 minutes  
**Plot points**: 6  
**Directorial sensibility**: Hirokazu Kore-eda, with the still-waters-run-deep female perspective of Chen Kun's *Mountains May Depart*

---

## Overall tone
- **Central idea**: When love tries to fill an absence, genuine creation comes from honestly questioning one's own existence.
- **Visual style**: Minimal everyday life, metaphorical empty space, restrained low saturation.
- **Narrative approach**: Nonlinear flashbacks; “creative time” and “intimate time” interwoven within a day.
- **Point of view**: First-person interior perspective, calmly observing her own behavior and others' reactions.
- **Music**: Ethereal piano, silence, slow progression, occasional minimal low strings.
- **Emotional arc**: Calm → anticipation → anxiety → inner exhaustion → resistance → self-consistency.
- **Devices**: The canvas repeatedly contrasts with real relationships. Each rejected invitation to intimacy corresponds to an unfinished drawing folded away. Repeated shots of a brush resting motionless on paper foreshadow events.

## Characters
| Name | Identity | Appearance | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lin Wan | Independent illustrator | 28, long slightly wavy hair, usually a beige-gray sweater, old copper stud in her left ear | Introverted, sensitive, expresses emotion through art; distant in intimacy, strong personal boundaries |
| Chen Yuan | Boyfriend | 30, neat clothes, gentle smile; likes arranging details of her life | Gentle idealist, wants understanding but cannot comprehend her silence and distance |

## Screenplay

### S01: Silence after dinner · INT. · Day ｜ [00:00–00:35]

*“He asked whether I'd been painting. I said I had. But I wasn't painting him. I was painting the nights I never spoke of.”*

On the sofa she holds the rice ball she has just finished eating. Twig-like fingers make two strokes in the air, as if answering an unanswered call.

Chen Yuan leans against his chair. “Want to see an exhibition this weekend?”

Her gaze seems to pass through glass, settling on the half-finished painting: a city at night on the left, blankness on the right, as though someone tore it open.

    LIN WAN (empty-eyed, slowly): Look. This city is a painting half erased.

*“I'm afraid that once I begin expressing myself, I can never return to that still moment that belongs only to me.”*

Sound: rain on glass, distant air-conditioning unit, a woman's voice in a TV advertisement: “Happiness needs no explanation.”

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Static shot, slight push toward the painting, then slow withdrawal.
> - Composition: Medium shot from a low side angle; painting at upper left unbalances the frame.
> - Lighting: Overhead and weak diffused light; cool, low contrast, oppressive.
> - Color: Cool white and low saturation, dominated by gray-blue estrangement.
> - Atmosphere: Silence, fatigue, repression.
> - Music: Slowly descending single piano notes with very faint tape friction.
> - Transition: Rice-ball close-up cuts into the living room; exit by enlarging part of the painting.
> - Narrative function: Establish daily life and emotional separation; plant the unfinished painting.

---

### S02: Alone in the studio · INT. · Night ｜ [00:36–01:10]

*“I never paint the landscapes they want to see. I paint what I want to become—even if nobody sees.”*

She stands before the canvas, charcoal hovering over paper, still for half a second. The point lands at the corner of the character for “night,” then lifts as though pulled away.

Her watch reads 7:48. The phone vibrates: “I'd like to hear you sing tonight.”

    LIN WAN (softly, mouth barely moving): I can't sing. But I can draw.

She sits and studies the drafts covering the wall: windowsills, rain, a figure walking toward a window. One corner is blacked out. In it, a profile has tear tracks on the cheek, but the tears have not fallen.

Sound: charcoal scraping paper, a distant subway arriving, one brief bark.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Slight handheld sway, fixed pullback, pan across the drawing wall.
> - Composition: Overhead with sidelight; drawings occupy one-third, she sits in a corner.
> - Lighting: Dim warm-yellow desk-lamp sidelight casts elongated, silent shadows.
> - Color: Yellow-orange grayed overall; gray-yellow self-defense.
> - Atmosphere: Solemn, lonely, confronting the self.
> - Music: Piano slowly repeats a fifth; rhythm nearly stalls.
> - Transition: Handheld cut from the rice ball to the studio; night outside gradually brightens.
> - Narrative function: Directly oppose creative desire and an invitation, reinforcing self-expression's value.

---

### S03: Avoiding the call · INT. · Day ｜ [01:11–01:45]

*“He says he loves me, but doesn't know when I'll suddenly fall silent—like a brush stopping halfway, then never moving again.”*

The phone rings. Her finger hovers over Answer. An unread message: “Have you been exhausting yourself lately?”

She breathes deeply, sets the phone down, and gently slides a sketch aside. Its figure has closed eyes; a note at their feet reads “Please do not disturb.”

    LIN WAN (softly, with a smile): I'm fine. I just want to be alone today.

She puts on silent headphones, yet hears her amplified heartbeat. Cut to her diary: “Love is gentle, but creation is absolute.”

Sound: phone disconnects after two rings, heartbeat, turning pages, wind through the door gap.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push to the phone, sketch close-up, quick cut to her profile with closed eyes.
> - Composition: Low-angle close-up; half a face emerges at the sketch's edge, symmetrically matching her.
> - Lighting: Fragmented daylight slopes through curtains, contrasting light and dark.
> - Color: Pale gray and white, predominantly cream; resistance and gentleness.
> - Atmosphere: Subtle, quiet tension and calm refusal.
> - Music: Piano rises for a single beat, then falls silent.
> - Transition: Phone screen cuts to the drawing's closed eyes; snowflakes fall outside on exit.
> - Narrative function: Clarify the pull between romantic dependency and the creative self.

---

### S04: Weekend gathering · EXT. · Day ｜ [01:46–02:30]

*“They say art is empathy. I only hide feelings in empty spaces, waiting for someone to fill them—or for them to leave on their own.”*

In a café corner, she holds a paper cup with dark floating dust at the bottom. People laugh and talk. Someone hands her an art book; she accepts it, then puts it down.

A girl draws a couple holding hands. Someone else draws a city statue. Lin Wan takes a sheet and writes: “I paint the moment I walk into rain.”

    LIN WAN (a quiet laugh): You draw them. I draw me.

She lowers her gaze to blank paper. The character for “wind” she meant to draw has been circled out in red.

Sound: laughter, cheerful folk music, occasional coffee-machine drips.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Handheld tracking, then a still page close-up; freeze on empty space.
> - Composition: Exterior medium shot; her corner separates her clearly from the crowd.
> - Lighting: Natural sidelight, bright but gentle, alternating highlights and shadows.
> - Color: Warm yellow and bright pink express the fatigue of being observed.
> - Atmosphere: Lively surface, inner distance.
> - Music: Steady, cheerful folk music runs against her emotion.
> - Transition: Coffee-cup close-up cuts to canvas; she turns and leaves, receding.
> - Narrative function: Social surroundings expose inner withdrawal and deepen the split between art and affection.

---

### S05: Invitation home · INT. · Night ｜ [02:31–03:20]

*“He thinks love means coming closer. But the closer he comes, the more I want to leave—like wind stopping by the window, suddenly unwilling to move.”*

The handle turns. She stands in a white sweater, her earring faintly shining. Chen Yuan waits outside with white flowers.

“I'll come home and keep you company while you finish this.”

She looks at the nighttime city painting, its right half blank.

    LIN WAN (slightly shaking her head): Finish this? I haven't finished myself yet.

She goes inside and gently returns the drawing paper to its place without opening it. Looking at him: “I want to be alone tonight.”

Sound: closing door, footsteps, flowers lightly swaying in wind, the sound of closing eyes.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push to the handle, hold on Chen Yuan, then follow her putting down the drawing.
> - Composition: Diagonal, the pair symmetrical with the painting between them.
> - Lighting: Warm interior light slants from the right, dividing light and dark.
> - Color: Warm yellow, predominantly cream; hesitation and refusal.
> - Atmosphere: Gentle restraint, a tender farewell.
> - Music: Background music abruptly disappears, leaving breath.
> - Transition: Enter on the turning handle; she turns her back and walks out of frame.
> - Narrative function: The key turn: rejecting intimacy to preserve the self.

---

### S06: Last painting · INT. · Morning ｜ [03:21–03:55]

*“At last I understand. Creation isn't about pleasing anyone. It proves that I once truly lived.”*

Morning light slants in. Before the easel, she faces an unmarked surface. She bends, picks up a brush, and places it in sunlight.

Looking at her old sketches, she suddenly smiles. She raises her hand and looks at the copper stud in her earlobe, as though confirming a memory's anchor.

Sound: wind, clear birdsong, sunlight's tiny sounds through leaves, faint paper rustle.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push to the earring, pull out to the whole studio, hold still.
> - Composition: Low angle from behind; easel centered.
> - Lighting: Bright natural sidelight, a golden halo around her; awakening.
> - Color: Golden yellow and morning blue, dominated by amber; calm and freedom.
> - Atmosphere: Clarity, release, self-confirmation.
> - Music: Piano slowly rises from single notes into chords, gentle as breathing.
> - Transition: Slow studio push-in; dissolve into a sunlit live-action long take.
> - Narrative function: Complete her choice. The canvas remains unfinished, but life is whole.
