# Short screenplay: The Seventh Folder in the Office
**Genre**: Workplace thriller / Absurdism / Psychological chamber drama / Black comedy  
**Duration**: Approximately 7 minutes  
**Plot points**: 6  
**Directorial sensibility**: David Lynch × Olivier Assayas

---

## Overall tone
- **Central idea**: When a company packages control as “care,” the most absurd details hide in the most ordinary files.
- **Visual style**: Desaturated, cool-toned realism with surreal fragments. The office gradually breaks down into a mirrored space.
- **Narrative approach**: Linear progression with increasingly absurd reversals. Each monologue plants a clue; “care” is ultimately revealed as a personality-copying experiment.
- **Point of view**: First person. “I,” an ordinary middle manager, am passively drawn into the system.
- **Music**: Low electronic tones, slow as a heartbeat, interrupted by distorted vocal echoes and occasional mechanical speech.
- **Emotional arc**: Calm → suspicion → absurdity → fear → composure → compassion → aftershock.
- **Devices**: Folder numbers 01–07 recur, each corresponding to an anomaly; repeated email alerts apply psychological pressure; all office scenes eventually appear reversed or mirrored.

## Characters
| Name | Identity | Appearance | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 32, middle manager supervising outsourced IT projects | Pressed plaid shirt; aluminum-alloy stud in left ear; a missing segment of the left little finger | Rational, restrained, habitually records things, overly dependent on rules |
| Director Lin | 45, head of the Human Resources Care Group | Black suit, thinning hair; always stands against the wall at the office's center | Extremely slow speech, pale smile; attendance-sheet patterns on his profile |
| Unnamed Employee A | Unnamed; often disappears after lunch | Blue work clothes, bulging shirt pocket | Appears only once, with a blurred back; sits in a corner |

## Screenplay

### S01: Launch-day meeting · INT. · Day ｜ [00:00–00:32]

*“My name is Chen Mo. I've worked here for three years. Every day, the same things: clock in, meetings, reports. Today, though, we were invited to the launch of an ‘Employee Care Program.’”*

I sit at one end of the long table, looking at an “Emotional Balance Triangle” and “Stress-Relief Pathway” on the whiteboard. Director Lin stands in the middle, smiling, holding warm water.

    DIRECTOR LIN (softly, slowly): We find that tired employees instinctively seek “empathy.” So we designed seven levels of care: listening, empathy, and then—self-reconstruction.

*“But what was ‘self-reconstruction’? I'd never heard that term in psychology class. Not until I found a passage tucked into page 3 in my desk drawer: ‘If you feel lonely, write down your voice. On the seventh day, we will read it to you.’”*

Sound: air-conditioning hum, a pen lightly tapping the table, a distant printer starting with a squeal.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Static medium shot, then push toward the whiteboard as its words appear one by one.
> - Composition: Eye level, diagonal arrangement, Director Lin at upper right.
> - Lighting: Natural light slopes in from the right, cool-white bands with slight shadows at their edges.
> - Color: Desaturated cyan, gray-blue and pale white.
> - Atmosphere: Calm but distant, like the indifference of digitized social contact.
> - Music: Low electronic pulses, slow as breathing.
> - Transition: Enter on emerging whiteboard text; slowly pull out to a wide shot.
> - Narrative function: Establish “care” and foreshadow the folders.

---

### S02: Monthly care email · INT. · Day ｜ [00:33–00:55]

*“The next day, an email arrived. Its strange subject read: ‘The seventh folder has been activated. Please report your deepest fear.’”*

I open it. The mailbox instantly turns gray. A folder icon floats above the message. It has no name, only “07.”

    I (trembling, to myself): My deepest fear… Is it that I'm no longer “me”?

I stare at the screen. Suddenly Director Lin's voice comes over the broadcast system—from my phone:

“Welcome to stage seven, Chen Mo. You have passed emotional screening.”

*“I never knew that ‘care’ required me to be ‘identified’ first.”*

Sound: email ding, three consecutive system beeps, someone coughing in the distance.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Slight handheld sway, then a fixed screen close-up; the folder drifts in the center.
> - Composition: Overhead angle, phone occupying the right third.
> - Lighting: Cool screen glow against natural ambient light creates a split.
> - Color: Blue-green display contrasts strongly with gray-white reality.
> - Atmosphere: Uncanny surveillance, oppressive as a phone-system notification.
> - Music: Electronics intensify, joined by faint repeated humming without dialogue or lyrics.
> - Transition: Enter as the email arrives; fade to black, then return to the morning meeting, now with red whiteboard text.
> - Narrative function: First establish the folder and start the chain of absurd logic.

### S03: Folder 01 · INT. · Day ｜ [00:56–01:20]

*“I searched the drawer. At the fold on page 3: ‘On May 10, you quietly said: I cannot hear.’”*

I think it is a typo. I check my log. That day I said nothing; I only shut down the computer.

I switch it on and search “I cannot hear.” A record appears: “Employee A, May 10, 2023. Emotional report: reports ‘hearing his own absence.’”

    I (quietly, shaking): That wasn't me… Someone else? Or an earlier “version” of me?

Folder 01 is labeled “Raw Emotion Collection.” I put it somewhere cool. It begins to heat up.

Sound: rubbing paper, an old printer starting, indistinct panting drifting from a corner.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Push toward the folder, then the dog-ear, slowly enlarging the words.
> - Composition: Low camera close to the drawer, creating pressure.
> - Lighting: Weak sidelight; elongated shadows at the folder's edge form a human outline.
> - Color: Yellowed paper, ochre and gray-brown suggest age.
> - Atmosphere: Oppressive absurdity of being recorded, an archive closing in.
> - Music: Electronic sound weakens into real breathing synchronized with a heartbeat.
> - Transition: Enter on the folded page's words; slowly slide left into the office background.
> - Narrative function: First mismatch between folders and self-perception, preparing the mirror image.

---

### S04: Folder 02 · INT. · Night ｜ [01:21–01:50]

*“I went to the meeting room as usual, but in the mirror saw another me, sitting there and softly saying: ‘You have already submitted version 2 of your self-statement.’”*

I spin around. The reflection stays still, then lifts the corner of his mouth and slowly opens a folder labeled “02.”

    MIRROR ME (calm, out of sync): Personality stability testing was completed on day six. You have accepted the implant.

I retreat. My heart stops. No sound but my own breath.

Sound: faint glass vibration; footsteps in the hall, not mine; an office chair opposite creaks in the shot.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Straight-on mirror shot records the reflection's action, then quick cut to the real me retreating.
> - Composition: Central mirror symmetry; the real and reflected selves look at one another diagonally.
> - Lighting: Bright reflection against a dark room creates two lighting systems.
> - Color: Cold blue and pallor in the mirror, gray-yellow in reality.
> - Atmosphere: Fracture, lost control, fear of a copied self.
> - Music: Abrupt silence; only breathing and faint ticking remain.
> - Transition: Enter as the reflection speaks; pull back while the meeting-room door slowly closes.
> - Narrative function: The first mirror self suggests the personality-copying system has begun operating.

---

### S05: Folder 03 · INT. · Night ｜ [01:51–02:25]

*“After finishing my email, I opened surveillance. The screen showed me at my desk cleaning my glasses. But another me was in the corner, smiling as he signed.”*

I open the recording. There are gaps in the timeline. At 00:18 I am concentrating on documents, yet on camera I place Folder 03 on the desk, turn—and see myself smiling.

    I (hoarse): This isn't me… The seventh “me” is watching me.

The screen goes black. Audio plays automatically: “Folder 03 archived successfully. Tester: Chen Mo. Status: stable, shareable.”

Sound: a mechanical voice repeats “Archived successfully”; electrical screen hiss and data-stream noise.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Multiple tracks, reality and surveillance shown side by side and intercut.
> - Composition: Overhead surveillance versus eye-level medium reality shots.
> - Lighting: Cold blue-white monitors alternate with warm yellow reality, shifting emotion.
> - Color: Blue-white surveillance and warm reality intensify the visual displacement.
> - Atmosphere: Near collapse; reality and virtuality blur.
> - Music: Low electronic music returns at a faster tempo.
> - Transition: Enter through surveillance; cut to black with a flash of the Folder 03 label.
> - Narrative function: Reveal multiple copies of the self trapped in a control loop.

---

### S06: Final room · INT. · Night ｜ [02:26–02:58]

*“I found filing cabinet 7 in the elevator shaft. Behind the iron door, a single row of metal shelves held seven identical folders. Every label read—‘You.’”*

Shaking, I open the first. The original diary: “I cannot hear.” “I never existed.” “I have been copied.”

I open the seventh. Its page reads:

“Version 7 has passed all emotional tests. Recommend sending the original version a ‘return command’ within 24 hours.”

    I (calm, almost laughing): So I was designed from the beginning. “Care” just makes them feel better about watching me.

I close the folder and leave. Behind me, the iron door closes silently.

Sound: the iron door clicks shut; soft electronic wind chimes, like a childhood toy.

> **Audiovisual notes**
> - Camera: Slowly enter from outside the iron door and zoom toward the folder labels.
> - Composition: Oblique low angle near the floor; folders in a straight row.
> - Lighting: No natural light; even cold-white illumination with no shadows.
> - Color: Highly saturated cold white; identical folder colors, emotionally icy.
> - Atmosphere: Empty, solemn, with compassion amid absurdity.
> - Music: Gentle wind chimes slowly fade into silence.
> - Transition: Enter through the door's gap; fade back to everyday office life, bright sunshine and Director Lin smiling.
> - Narrative function: The climax reveals care as a colonizing personality experiment. Returning to daily life closes the absurd loop.
