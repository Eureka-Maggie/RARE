Abstract

Open-ended generation has no automatic verifier of overall quality: a response must satisfy several requirements, and many different responses can be valid. Rubric-based reinforcement learning makes these requirements explicit, but fixed criteria can lose their ability to distinguish outputs as the policy improves.

RARE uses current-policy feedback to update criteria that are fully satisfied across a query’s outputs, while retaining the other criteria and their scores. Replacements assess remaining quality differences. On three script-generation tasks, targeting observed flaws gives a mean gain of 3.82 points over matched static rubrics. The release includes training code and 5,989 query–rubric pairs.

Method

For each query, RARE samples eight outputs and scores them against the current rubric. A criterion is saturated when all eight valid judgments assign its maximum score. RARE replaces up to three saturated criteria, rescores the replacements on the same outputs, and uses the updated rewards in the current policy update.

The sample-level variants differ in the replacement criteria: Negative targets observed flaws, Positive describes desired qualities, and Mixed combines both. Higher reward scores always indicate better quality.

Results

Dynamic rubrics improve their matched static baselines across all three script-generation tasks. Sample-level RARE with Negative replacements achieves the largest mean gain: 3.82 points over Sample-Static.

RL results average four training seeds. Mean averages the three task scores.
